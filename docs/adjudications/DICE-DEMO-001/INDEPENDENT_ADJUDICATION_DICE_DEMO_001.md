## 3. Source audit

### 3.1 v1/v2: global failure-state access (confirmed)

- v1 `local_recover(agents, tasks, adj, failed, …)` receives the complete `failed` set. It uses it to choose detectors (`x not in failed`) and to prune search (`if nb in failed or nb in seen: continue`).
- v2 `local(agents, tasks, adj, failed, …)` does the same, and also tries two detectors.

In both versions, dead neighbors are skipped **at zero message cost and zero search budget**, which is a free global liveness oracle. This explains most of the gap between the v2 multiring figure (MSR 0.9989) and v3 (0.978). In v3, about 22% of probes hit dead nodes and consume the visit budget.

**Disposition:** the v1/v2 numbers are valid for their own code. As evidence for "no global failure-state knowledge" they are **STRUCTURALLY_INVALID**. The package's own correction (DICE-DEMO-001-CORRECTION-001) is accurate.

### 3.2 v3: candidate visibility (confirmed, with conditions)

Static AST audit of `recover_one`:

- **Names referenced:** `net, detector, task, radius, max_visits, q, seen, u, h, v, st, deque, len`. It does **not** reference `failed`, `adj`, or `states`.
- **Attribute access:** only the public `LocalNetwork` API (`local_state`, `neighbors`, `probe`, `send`, `try_claim`, `messages`, `ledger`). It never touches private fields (`_adj`, `_states`).
- `neighbors(u)` returns only `u`'s adjacency.
- `probe` and `send` raise `NON_LOCAL_PROBE` / `NON_LOCAL_SEND` if the destination is not a direct edge, and neither was ever raised in 120 runs.
- `local_state(u)` raises if `u` is dead.
- `try_claim` reads and mutates only the visited node's own role/load/capacity.
- The candidate never calls a topology or population lookup during recovery.

All five checks in instructions Step 2 are **satisfied** for the recovery decision path.

**The conditions**, i.e. oracles at the candidate boundary:

1. **`local_detection_events` (disclosed by the package).** This reads the global `failed` set and `states[v].alive`, then hands the lowest-ID live neighbor a `(detector, Task)` pair. That amounts to a *perfect* failure detector in the Chandra–Toueg class-P sense: strong completeness, strong accuracy, zero latency, zero message cost, and no false suspicion.
2. **`LocalNetwork.probe` (NOT disclosed).** Each probe returns ground-truth `self._states[dst].alive` instantly. It is a second perfect-liveness oracle, used 6,445 times per 1,000-agent random run. A real probe to a dead peer costs a timeout and can be wrong when a live peer is slow.
3. **Task-metadata transfer (NOT disclosed).** The detection event carries the failed owner's `Task` (id and required role). The detector could only know this if task state had been replicated to neighbors beforehand. That replication is neither modeled nor counted.

### 3.3 Adjudication: is the injected detection event sufficient?

The package asks for choice A (acceptable abstraction) or B (too strong). My ruling splits by claim:

- **A, for one narrow claim.** In `SYNTHETIC_EXECUTION`, the abstraction is acceptable for showing that the *recovery decision logic* needs no global failure list or topology query, *given* a perfect local failure detector and pre-replicated task metadata. Separating detection from recovery in this way is standard practice.
- **B, for everything else.** It is insufficient for any claim about detection itself: detection latency, false suspicion, detection cost, completeness under correlated/regional failure, recovery time, or end-to-end message complexity. It is also insufficient for an unconditional "no global dependency" claim, because two oracles, not one, sit at the candidate boundary (§3.2 items 1–2).

Therefore:

```text
STRICT_LOCAL_RECOVERY_DECISION (given perfect local FD + replicated task metadata) = PASS_WITHIN_SCOPE
STRICT_LOCAL_RECOVERY_WITH_REAL_FAILURE_DETECTION                                = NOT_ESTABLISHED
REAL_FAILURE_DETECTION                                                          = NOT_ESTABLISHED
```

Every future statement of the v3 result should carry the qualifier "assuming a perfect local failure detector and neighbor-replicated task metadata."

## 5. Perturbation locality (DICE-MET-011): package's negative result examined

**The as-coded value is verified:** `perturbation_locality` = 0.9937 / 0.9928 / 0.9590 at n=1,000. The "touches ~96–99%" statement is correct *for that field*.

**However, that field is not the TDD metric.** DICE-MET-011 is defined as the "fraction of agents whose control/coordination **state changes** following a **localized** perturbation", and DICE-ADAPT-008 uses the same wording. The v3 field is the union of every node read, probed, or messaged across all 200 concurrent recoveries, which measures contact footprint, not state change. In addition, 20% random and 20% high-degree loss are not localized perturbations at all.

The quantity was measured three ways:

| Reading (n=1,000) | Random | Targeted HD | Local cut |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| As coded: union of contacted nodes | 0.994 | 0.993 | 0.959 |
| Per-recovery footprint, mean nodes (fraction of N) | 33.2 (3.3%) | 33.1 (3.3%) | 32.6 (3.3%) |
| State change, claims only (load mutated) | 0.178 | 0.179 | 0.152 |
| State change, claims + nodes receiving `search_forward` | 0.792 | 0.792 | 0.789 |
| **Small localized perturbation:** 1% local cut (10 agents) | as-coded 0.225 | — | — |
| 5% local cut (50 agents) | as-coded 0.703 | — | — |

**Adjudication:**

- Each individual recovery is bounded, at about 33 nodes with a cap of 81. The ~99% figure is mostly an aggregation effect of 200 simultaneous recoveries.
- Even so, the result is **not localized** under any reading. A 10-agent local cut still reaches 22% of a 1,000-agent collective, about 22 contacted agents per failed agent. Counting forwarded-request state, ~79% of agents change coordination state under 20% loss.
- The as-coded metric does not implement MET-011, so it can support neither a positive finding nor a clean FAIL.

```text

  (metric not implemented per TDD definition; all computed readings indicate non-local
   blast radius; the high MSR must not be read as a locality result)
```

A preregistered MET-011 specialization is needed, per TDD §18 ("MUST declare the specialization before execution"). It should state which state fields count, and it should be run under a genuinely localized perturbation (e.g. a 1% cut).

## 6. "1,000-agent-equivalent"

Recovery runs sequentially in task-ID order in a single thread, and each claim completes atomically before the next recovery starts. There is no concurrency, contention, message latency, or message loss.

## 7. Message accounting (DICE-MET-004/005)

### 7.1 What v3 counts (n=1,000, random)

| Counted item | Share of messages | Comment |
|---|---:|---|
| `neighbor_probe` | 50.4% | one-way; the reply is free and instant |
| `search_forward` | 39.4% | real inter-agent message |
| `local_candidate_check` | 8.8% | **over-count**: a node reading its own state is not a network message |
| claim | 1.4% | local mutation; no award or ack message exists |

The decomposition was checked to sum exactly to `recovery_messages` in every run.

### 7.2 What v3 omits (material)

1. **All failure-detection traffic** (heartbeats, SWIM ping / ping-req, suspicion dissemination) is counted as zero. In a real system this is steady-state O(N) per protocol period and continues outside recovery windows.
2. **Probe replies:** every probe receives an oracle answer at no cost. Counting request plus response would roughly add another 50% of messages.
3. **Result propagation:** nothing tells the detector, the task's dependents, or any registry that a new owner exists, and there is no award/ack round.
4. **Task-state handoff and replication:** the pre-replication that lets a neighbor know the failed owner's task is neither modeled nor counted (§3.2 item 3).

The omitted coordination traffic is material, so **DICE-MET-004 and DICE-MET-005 are downgraded to PARTIAL** per instructions Step 7. This also bears on DICE-SCL-009 ("mechanism traffic included"), which is not in the manifest.

### 7.3 On the slope ≈ 1

The slope is **structural**, not an empirical discovery. Per failed task, messages are bounded by the `max_visits` cap: at most ≈ 81 checks + 81 probes + 81 forwards + 1 claim. Failures are a fixed 20% of N. Total recovery messages are therefore O(N) *by construction* in this model. Measured messages per failed task are flat across the ladder (58.7 → 63.4 random), which fits that bound.

The bound is bought with completion. Sensitivity at n=1,000, varying only the `run_one` parameter (source untouched):

| max_visits | Reassign (random) | Msgs/task | Reassign (local cut) |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 40 | 0.727 | 46.3 | 0.622 |
| **80 (as run)** | **0.890** | **63.9** | **0.758** |
| 160 | 0.984 | 73.6 | 0.838 |
| 320 | 1.000 | 75.5 | 0.845 (detection ceiling) |

The random/targeted shortfall comes from the budget parameter: the `seen` budget is consumed by probes, including dead ones, and unrecovered tasks performed only ~13 candidate checks. It is not a limit of the mechanism, and 100% completion is reachable at ~18% more messages. The local-cut shortfall is **not** a budget problem; it is the 84.5% detection ceiling from §4.

Conclusion: the O(N) behavior holds analytically for this abstract model. It does not establish DICE-SCL-006 or ASYMPTOTIC_O_N, because detection and reply traffic are excluded and the Phase-3 condition (33% failure, 100K agents, Byzantine/compromise) was never exercised.

## 10. Required conclusions

**1. Strongest claim independently supported.**
In a deterministic synthetic model of 1,000 logical agents on a degree-≤8 multiring, a recovery procedure whose decision path reads only visited-node local state and direct-neighbor probes reassigns 89% (random), 89% (high-degree-targeted) and 76% (localized-cut) of tasks orphaned by 20% crash loss, with zero role mismatches. It uses ~54–64 counted messages per failed task, and the count holds flat from 250 to 2,000 agents. This holds *given a perfect local failure detector and neighbor-replicated task metadata*. The result reproduces byte-for-byte across Python runtimes.

**2. Strongest claim refuted.**
- The v2 headline of "~99.9% random / 96.8% localized-cut mission success" as evidence of local recovery without global failure knowledge is refuted. v2's candidate reads the global failed set and skips dead neighbors for free. (The package already concedes this; I confirm it.)
- The framing "97.8% mission success" is misleading as a headline, because 80 of those points are unconditional. The candidate recovers 89%, not 97.8%, of affected work.
- The package's own negative claim, "touches 96–99% of the network", is numerically correct but measures the wrong quantity. It should not be cited as a DICE-MET-011 result in either direction.

**3. Hidden assumptions.**

- A perfect, free, zero-latency failure detector (disclosed).
- `probe()` is a second perfect liveness oracle (**undisclosed**).
- Task metadata known to the detector without replication cost (**undisclosed**).
- Sequential, atomic, contention-free recoveries with no message loss or delay.
- Crash-stop failures all at t0, with no failures during recovery.
- Unrecovered tasks are silently dropped; no retry and no escalation.
- `local_candidate_check` counted as a network message (inflates), while replies, awards, and detection are free (deflates).
- MSR floor of 0.80.
- The role-binding metric is tautological.
- Detection is always delegated to the lowest-ID live neighbor.

**4. Next falsifying experiment** (extends DICE-DEMO-WO-001).
Replace `local_detection_events` and the oracle in `probe()` with a real SWIM-style detector running on a discrete-event clock: per-link latency and loss, ping / ping-req / suspicion timeouts, and counted heartbeat traffic. Add a real replication step for task metadata (k neighbor replicas, counted). Then:

- (a) Preregister MET-011 as "agents whose load, ownership, or request-state changed" and run a 1% localized cut. **Falsifier:** > 5% of agents change state.
- (b) Raise `max_visits` until random-loss MET-018 ≥ 0.99, then re-fit slopes including detection traffic over 250→16,000. **Falsifier:** slope > 1.15 or MET-018 decays with N.
- (c) Local cut with replicas held outside the failed ball. **Falsifier:** completion stays at the ≈84.5% detection ceiling.
- (d) Inject false suspicions (live-but-slow nodes). **Falsifier:** any duplicate ownership.

**5. Credible DARPA-call artifact?**
**Yes, but only as a research-apparatus and method artifact, not as a capability claim.** What is credible:

- the reproducibility discipline (hash-pinned, byte-identical cross-runtime reproduction);
- the voluntary exposure of the v1/v2 oracle leak;
- the TDD traceability;
- a clean separation of candidate and comparator.

These show evaluation hygiene of the kind a DICE TA1/TA3 reviewer looks for. It is **not** credible as evidence of DICE capability at 1,000 agents. The agents are not LLMs; detection is oracular; locality is not established; and the accounting omits the traffic most likely to dominate at scale. It must be presented with the §10.1 claim verbatim, the qualifiers in §3.3, and MET-018 (not MSR) as the headline. Anything stronger would not survive review.
